# Shoppers Drug Mart: mission, strategy and customers

Research gathered 2026-11-19. It checks the dashboard against what Shoppers Drug Mart (SDM) and Loblaw say they are trying to do, and records what changed as a result. Background on formats and history is in [02](02-sdm-store-formats.md); Loblaw's banners are in [01](01-loblaw-banners.md).

## Mission (Verified)

- SDM's stated promise is to help Canadians **"Live Life Well"** [1], [2]. Executives use the same words for the purpose of its health programs: "Our purpose is to help Canadians live life well and that means providing the tools they need to successfully manage their overall health" [3].
- Loblaw, the parent since 2014, uses the same line for the whole group [2].
- An older "vision" quoted on third-party sites ("the leader in helping Canadians discover a healthier outlook on life") could not be confirmed on an SDM page, so it is not used here (To verify).

## Strategy: where the growth is

**1. Pharmacy and health care services are the engine (Verified).**

| Period | Pharmacy and health care services, same-store sales | Front store, same-store sales | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2024 | +6.3% | weaker, partly offsetting | [4] |
| Q3 2025 | +5.9% | +1.9% (beauty and OTC; low-margin electronics exited) | [5] |
| 2025 | +6.0%; segment sales about $9.94 billion, up 8.2% | cosmetics and OTC held up | [6] |
| Q2 2026 | +7.5% | +1.3% | [7], [8] |

- SDM opened 78 **Pharmacy Care Clinics** in 2024 and Loblaw planned about 100 more in 2025, as part of a roughly $10 billion five-year investment [9], [10]. Loblaw ran more than 150 clinics by the end of 2024 [11].
- Services named in Loblaw's reporting: specialty pharmacy, pharmacist-led clinical services such as **medication reviews**, minor ailment assessments and vaccines [6].
- A secondary report puts SDM at 1.7 million minor-ailment consultations in 2025, over 85% booked for the same or next day [12] (To verify against Loblaw's own disclosure).

**2. British Columbia opened the door in 2023 (Verified).** Since 1 June 2023, BC pharmacists can assess and prescribe for 21 minor ailments (including acne, pink eye, rashes, hives, menstrual pain, indigestion, headaches, shingles and urinary tract infections) and for contraception [13], [14]. BC was the last province to do this [15]. SDM tells British Columbians to book these online, in the PC Health app or in store [16]. Surrey alone was set to get seven SDM Pharmacy Care Clinics [17].

**3. The front store is held, not grown (Verified, with one Inferred step).**
- Front-store growth comes from **beauty and OTC**; SDM has exited low-margin electronics [5].
- Own brands: **Life Brand** (since 1962) and **Quo Beauty** (launched October 2020, over 1,000 products, its biggest beauty line) [18], [19]. Loblaw's **no name** and **PC** lines also sell in SDM.
- Beauty: SDM calls itself Canada's leading beauty and pharmacy retailer, with Beauty Boutique prestige departments [19], [20] (self-reported).
- Food: in 2026 Loblaw began a **food refresh** in selected SDM stores: more products at lower prices, with "convenience, value and multicultural products". 17 stores were done by September 2026, 43 expected by year end. Loblaw says SDM is not becoming a discount grocer [8], [21].
- Inferred: SDM wants the front store to keep a shopper's basket (beauty, OTC, everyday basics, top-up food) while the pharmacy brings the visit.

**4. PC Optimum ties it together (Verified).** Over 18 million members, formed in 2018 by merging Shoppers Optimum and PC Plus [22]. Loblaw uses its data to personalise offers and sell supplier media (Loblaw Media) [11].

## Main customers

No public source gives SDM's customer profile by age, sex or income. What can be said:

- **Patients**: everyone who fills a prescription, weighted toward older people. SDM planned its growth around ageing Canadians as far back as 2009 [02, source 4] (Verified).
- **Beauty shoppers, mostly women**: SDM's beauty launches and its women's health programs (Shoppers LOVE YOU) target women [19], [23] (Inferred from targeting, not from a customer split).
- **Convenience trips**: urban stores with long hours, for top-ups and grab-and-go (see [02](02-sdm-store-formats.md)).
- **PC Optimum members**: nearly two-thirds of Canadian adults [22], so membership says little about who a store's shoppers are; the purchase data would.

## Gap review of the dashboard

| Area | Before | Gap against the strategy | Change made |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stock notes | Every call was about shelves. Pharmacy services appeared only inside the seniors note | Services are SDM's fastest-growing line and BC has allowed pharmacist prescribing since 2023. The dashboard told a manager what to shelve but not which service to lead with | Each store now gets a **pharmacy services call** from its census: medication reviews where seniors are many, minor ailments and contraception where residents are young or on student permits, same-day care for families' common ailments, and the standard list elsewhere. It is the first item on the Stock notes tab |
| What to stock: Pharmacy row | The same sentence at all 29 stores ("compete on services") | Not specific to the store | The row now names the service the census points to |
| Own brands | Life Brand and no name in the price calls | Quo Beauty (SDM's own beauty line) and PC Optimum offers were missing where they fit best | Young areas: Quo Beauty named next to trend cosmetics. Price-sensitive areas: PC Optimum points offers added to the call |
| Food | Fresh grocery "Avoid" next to a supermarket; grab-and-go where no meal counter is close | Already in line: SDM is not becoming a grocer, and its food refresh adds value and multicultural products | No change. The language note's call to carry familiar food brands matches the refresh |
| Electronics | Not a category here | SDM is exiting low-margin electronics | No change needed |
| Synthetic sales mix | Pharmacy 44% (Wesbrook) and 30% (campus store) | Loblaw's 2025 pharmacy and services sales (about $9.94 billion) are a little under half of drug retail, so the mix is plausible | No change |
| Customer segments | Census-based, with a note to confirm with PC Optimum | Matches how Loblaw would actually do it | No change |

### Not changed, and why

- **Clinic locations**: SDM does not publish a list of which Vancouver stores have a Pharmacy Care Clinic in a form that could be checked, so the map does not mark them (To verify).
- **Food refresh stores**: Loblaw has not named them, so no store is tagged.
